Photographs

A few from years on the road across the US, unedited — straight out of the camera, shot on a Sony α7 III. What's below is just a preview; fill out the form below to get access to the full-resolution JPG and archive-quality TIFF originals, for anyone who'd like to learn from them, not just look at them — I respond to every request.

I'm still digging through my archive — expect new photographs added here regularly over few weeks from the day of publish of website.

A note on file format: to protect privacy while preserving archival quality, every original is provided as a 16-bit, lossless TIFF rather than the native Sony .ARW. Sony locks permanent hardware signatures — the camera's serial number and exact shutter count — deep inside that proprietary raw container, and exiftool's write-safety checks refuse to alter them on any raw file. So each ARW is demosaiced into a TIFF using darktable, a plain container exiftool can then strip completely. You still get the full sensor resolution and the same underlying image data — no crop, no color grading, no retouching — just migrated out of Sony's closed container into an open, privacy-clean format. The only tradeoff is size: expect roughly 115MB per file, versus ~49MB for the original ARW.

Why this exists

A personal project — not a nonprofit, not a campaign.

These photographs are mine — shot over years of traveling across the US, nothing to do with Nepal. They were just sitting in an archive. Right now, people in Nepal need urgent help, and giving these away felt like the right way to turn that archive into something useful, even indirectly.

Please take them, use them, learn from them — and if you're able to, give through one of the Nepal relief links around this site. Not to me.

How donations work

The photographs are free to take. Giving to Nepal happens somewhere else entirely — through one of two separate links, linked wherever a donation box appears, never a photograph download, never a page on this site.

I'm not a charity, and I'm not affiliated with the Government of Nepal or the nonprofit fundraiser — I don't organize or moderate either one.

Money collected here: $0.

What the two links actually are

One is a GoFundMe fundraiser benefiting American Society of Nepalese Engineers (ASNEngr), a registered US nonprofit with a track record helping Nepal before — the 2015 earthquake, the 2019 floods — not a page I built or control. They forward what's raised to Nepal's official Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund. Someone else set the campaign up; I just found it, checked the nonprofit's status independently, and decided it was solid enough to point people toward.

The other is the Government of Nepal's own Ministry of Home Affairs page for that same Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund — no intermediary, run by the government itself, with bank transfer, card and QR options listed there directly. Nothing routed through anyone else, but no US tax receipt either.

What the numbers mean

Each donation button carries its own click count, shown just below it, so the two links never blend into one number. Those are clicks from this site — not completed donations. I don't ask anyone how much they gave.

What I don't claim

I don't know how much visitors ultimately donate, unless verified aggregate information is later provided by the nonprofit, GoFundMe, or the Nepal government.

Donations made through the GoFundMe fundraiser are generally tax-deductible for US donors, since the nonprofit is a registered 501(c)(3) and GoFundMe issues an automatic receipt. Donations made directly through Nepal's own government page are not — that's the tradeoff between the two links. Still worth confirming with a tax professional for your own situation.
